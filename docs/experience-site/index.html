A queue of questions worth answering

The card gives one decision a clear focus. Its controls should fit that decision: a quick tag, a transfer comparison, or a searchable repayment allocation. A card can represent a transaction, a pair, or a whole set affected by one problem.

The input inventory

Based on code and documentation. These are potential tasks, not counts from your private accounts.

Import exceptions

When your input helps

Account routing is unknown/ambiguous; an export failed; duplicate-looking rows need interpretation.

The question and surface

Which account? Is this an additional payment? A file summary or side-by-side comparison, linking to Snapshots for technical failures.

Resolved when

A valid account/duplicate decision is saved, or the source problem is corrected and processing succeeds. One file problem should not create hundreds of transaction cards.

Transfers

When your input helps

A plausible own-account pair needs confirmation, several candidates compete, or a linked pair has an unexplained difference.

The question and surface

Are these the same movement? Show both accounts, dates, amounts and difference together, with candidate choices.

Resolved when

Explicit Link; or an explicit decision that a suggestion is not a transfer. Rejection memory is a proposed capability, not established behavior.

Expense tagging

When your input helps

A non-transfer expense has no tag or the user has opened it for correction.

The question and surface

What did I buy? This is the strongest fit for quick card sorting. Offer a split editor for mixed purchases.

Resolved when

Valid tag portions are saved. The broad category follows the tag's parent; it is not a second classification chore.

Incoming purpose

When your input helps

Money received is neither a linked transfer nor fully explained as income/repayment.

The question and surface

What is this money for? Visible choices for income type or expense reimbursement, with transfer inspection when relevant.

Resolved when

Purpose is saved; any remaining amount stays explicit rather than being silently treated as allocated. An income tag alone does not set purpose today.

Repayment allocation

When your input helps

A receipt is meant to reimburse spending, but target expenses or amounts need selection.

The question and surface

Which costs does this cover? Keep the receipt as an anchor and open a searchable multi-selection workspace with an allocation summary. Events select unique expense leaves.

Resolved when

The person and allocation are saved within receipt/expense capacities. A remainder remains unresolved or is explicitly handled under a future agreed policy.

Shared-cost agreement

When your input helps

The user knows an expense was shared and needs to record who owes what, or amend an existing agreement.

The question and surface

Who is responsible for this cost? People and exact split controls, with existing repayments visible.

Resolved when

Agreed shares are saved. Tagging or recognizing a person cannot establish these shares. Ordinary personal purchases need no extra confirmation.

Event organization

When your input helps

The user is organizing a trip/event, or choosing to inspect a relevant date suggestion.

The question and surface

Which transactions belong here? An event-centered calendar/list with bulk selection is better than a compulsory card for every transaction.

Resolved when

Selected memberships are saved. Being outside all events is valid. Event membership by itself never allocates a repayment.

Automation and naming conflicts

When your input helps

Aliases overlap, rules propose competing mappings, or an existing-data preview is ready to apply.

The question and surface

Which definition should apply? A grouped exception card can open Organize's existing preview/change/apply tools.

Resolved when

The conflicting definition is corrected and the affected set is rechecked, or the approved preview is applied. Avoid one identical card per affected row.

Unassigned does not always mean unfinished

No event, no alias or no shared split is often perfectly valid. Already-recorded money owed can be waiting rather than actionable. A linked transfer should never become an untagged expense or income chore.

Ordinary transactions already handled by a rule should reduce your work, not generate another confirmation ritual.

What clearing a card should mean

  • A specific decision was saved. Tagging does not settle a debt. Event membership does not allocate a repayment.
  • Failures and cancellations stay put. The envelope celebrates a successful save, not an attempted one.
  • Later is not resolved. Postponing, dismissing suggestions and undoing decisions need explicit behavior; a universal “reviewed” flag would conceal remaining questions.
Example: $120 dinner, $60 share, $70 repayment

Illustrative only. The dinner is tagged Restaurants and Alex's agreed share is $60. Neither decision needs asking again.

When Alex sends $70, the useful question is which costs it covers. Allocate $60 to the dinner; the remaining $10 stays visible.

The current app can retain an unassigned remainder. Exactly how to explain that $10 as extra income or another purpose remains a product decision. The dinner does not need an event to be properly recorded.

Two accounting interactions still need a decision

Mixed receiptsHow should a payment reimburse expenses and also contain explicitly explained extra money?
Merchant refundsHow should a refund relate to its original purchase, separately from a friend's reimbursement?

The next useful trial

Keep the current card shell and try three actual decisions: tag a purchase, compare a transfer pair, and allocate a repayment. Include ambiguity, a split purchase, excess money and a failed save. Test whether the work is clear and satisfying, before more animation tuning.

Earlier sorting checkpoint

Make sorting feel continuous

You like the card-sorting idea. The open question is how to make the whole gesture flow without careful steering or surprise jumps.

  1. Pick up the cardA clear distinction between tapping to edit and dragging to sort.
  2. Enter a categoryReveal its tags while keeping the gesture understandable.
  3. Move onto a tagA stable, readable target and an unmistakable drop preview.
  4. Release and saveShow what is being saved and keep the current context until it succeeds.
  5. Continue predictablyAdvance after the first assignment. Stay put when editing an already tagged card.
Cancel or miss the target → stayNo assignment and no queue jump.
Save fails → retain contextKeep the correction or retry easy to understand.
Leave the tag circle → close itPreserve the agreed behavior; inspect the boundary transitions.
Tap the card → edit a splitSave / Cancel remain deliberate alternatives to the quick gesture.

A small, realistic trial

Use synthetic cards: a simple tag, a crowded category, an existing assignment, a two-tag split, a cancelled drag and a delayed or failed save. Try the phone equivalents too.

Look for accidental choices, collapsing targets, backtracking, unclear saves and unexpected navigation. Then refine the transition that actually causes friction.

What we observed—and what is still a hypothesis

Source inspection found immediate hover expansion, closing at the circle boundary, and a card-position reset before the asynchronous save finishes. These are places to observe, not confirmed causes of the jankiness.

The phone layout hides the sorter's local status area, creating another feedback difference to evaluate. Functional tests cover saving and navigation, but do not establish fluidity. No new live interaction test was run for this checkpoint.

Still outside the finished loop

Known boundaries, not a list of new features to chase right now.

  • Bank export automation and a supported Wealthsimple importer.
  • Full month-coverage and balance reconciliation; snapshot presence alone does not prove completeness.
  • Automatic transfer linking during import. Today it remains an explicit testing/apply workflow.
  • A general backup/restore interface; private recovery copies already exist.
  • Confirmed hands-on phone comfort. Offline editing and home-screen installation are outside the first mobile pass.

Earlier proposed focus order

  1. Expenses · gesture, feedback and correction
  2. Income & shared costs · allocations, shares and repayments
  3. Transfers & Events · pairing and optional grouping
  4. Overview & Dashboard · useful answers and drilldowns
  5. Organize & Snapshots · repeated cleanup and exceptions

Test mobile alongside each area. This is a recommendation for discussion, not a locked roadmap.
